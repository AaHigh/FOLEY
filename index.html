The Foley case, checked against the record — and what the “1% reward” really is

David Foley’s 72-month sentence is real. The wire-fraud conviction is not — a jury acquitted him of it. And the restitution question, the one that decides whether victims actually get paid, is still not publicly settled. Here is what the court record, DOJ, and the written program terms actually say.

Bottom line: For a named victim in this case, the reward programs are a dead end — the case is already charged, tried, and sentenced, and it is a securities case, not the health-care/benefits fraud the new program covers. The real channel is criminal restitution through the U.S. Attorney’s Office in Chicago, and it has to be claimed actively. The steps are in Section 4.

1

Your account, checked against the record

Five claims from the victim’s account, tested against court filings, DOJ releases, and dated news reports. Four hold up in whole or in part. One — the conviction label — does not, and the correction matters for restitution.

“He’s going to do 72 months” Confirmed

Sentenced to 72 months (6 years) on Wednesday, August 19, 2026, by Judge Steven C. Seeger. Law360, Aug. 19, 2026; Al Lewis, Business Blunders, Aug. 21, 2026.

“He was convicted of wire fraud” Corrected

He was charged with wire fraud and acquitted of all 8 wire-fraud counts (Counts 1–8, 18 U.S.C. § 1343). On November 4, 2024 the jury convicted him on 2 securities-fraud counts — Count 9 (15 U.S.C. §§ 78j(b), 78ff; Rule 10b-5) and Count 10 (15 U.S.C. §§ 77q(a), 77x). The distinction matters: his only convictions being Title 15 offenses is exactly what made restitution legally contested. Defendant’s post-trial motion, Dkt. 234, U.S. v. Foley.

“A promissory note was the crux of the case” Confirmed

The government’s admitted-exhibit list includes the September 2, 2014 promissory note from HVEL to David Maidenbaum, its amendment, and its January 28, 2015 assignment to Foley. At sentencing, Judge Seeger described Foley and Blankenship as having “created something out of thin air” by creating fake debt for NTGL. The SEC separately alleged Foley disclosed only $60,500 of $495,000 in convertible notes/debentures issued to him or his assignees. Dkt. 189 exhibit list; Law360 sentencing report; SEC complaint (2019).

“He reports to prison in late October 2026” Unverified

No DOJ release, judgment, or reputable news report found states a surrender date or a facility for this sentence — there is no DOJ sentencing press release for this case at all. Forum speculation is contradictory (one post guessed custody at MCC Chicago; another guessed self-report within ~60 days, which would land mid-to-late October). Plausible, but not confirmed publicly. A victim’s VNS notice or the written judgment would settle it.

“The case took roughly 10 years” Mostly right

Measured from the alleged scheme (March 2013 – October 2016), the road to sentencing ran over a decade. Measured from the indictment (January 11, 2021) to sentencing, it was about 5½ years — with sentencing itself repeatedly continued from early 2025 and heard in installments.

The case: United States v. Foley

A pump-and-dump in NanoTech Gaming (NTGL) shares — run, the government alleged and the SEC charged, partly from inside a federal prison while Foley served time for the earlier UltraCade fraud.

Case
United States of America v. David Foley, et al.

Hon. Steven C. Seeger

Bennie Blankenship — pleaded guilty April 2022; testified as a cooperating witness

What the government alleged

From March 2013 to October 2016, Foley acquired NanoTech Gaming Inc. (NTGL) shares, caused them to be issued without restrictive legends using falsified opinion letters, Blankenship touted them, and the pumped shares were sold to the public through River North Equity. DOJ noted that during part of the scheme Foley was incarcerated in California on the earlier, unrelated conviction (Section 5). DOJ, U.S. Attorney’s Office N.D. Ill., indictment release; Dkt. 234.

The verdict, count by count

  • COUNTS 1–8 — Wire fraud, 18 U.S.C. § 1343 → NOT GUILTY on all eight.
  • COUNT 9 — Securities fraud, 15 U.S.C. §§ 78j(b), 78ff and Rule 10b-5 → GUILTY.
  • COUNT 10 — Securities fraud, 15 U.S.C. §§ 77q(a), 77x → GUILTY.

Trial began October 15, 2024; verdict November 4, 2024. Sentence: 72 months, imposed August 19, 2026. Whether Foley was taken into custody at sentencing or allowed to self-surrender, and whether he has appealed, could not be confirmed from accessible sources. Dkt. 234; Law360, Aug. 19, 2026.

Restitution: the open question

This is the number that matters most to victims — and it is the one number no public source settles. Do not quote any figure below as “the restitution amount.” They are pre-sentencing loss estimates the government argued over, not a confirmed court order.

Could not verify: whether the August 19, 2026 judgment ordered restitution at all, the total if it did, the per-victim list, or a deferred-restitution date. No judgment text, DOJ sentencing release, or reputable news report stating any of it was found; Law360’s full sentencing article is paywalled.

Why it was fought over

Restitution was contested and legally awkward. Foley’s own motion to continue sentencing (Dkt. 265, filed May 13, 2025) noted his objections had to address “the statutory bases for restitution when a defendant is convicted of a Title 15 offense, the definition of a ‘victim,’ the level of causality required” — and that it is rare for a defendant’s only convictions to be securities-fraud statutes without wire-fraud or conspiracy convictions. That is a direct consequence of the wire-fraud acquittals. If restitution was deferred at sentencing because losses weren’t yet ascertainable, federal practice (18 U.S.C. § 3664(d)(5)) is for the court to set a final determination date — generally within 90 days after sentencing. Whether Judge Seeger did so is not confirmed. Dkt. 265 (RECAP archive)

Loss figures argued at sentencing — government estimates, not a restitution order

All figures cover NTGL trading losses in the December 2015 – August 2016 window unless noted. The court questioned the calculation and ordered supplemental submissions.

Government’s final position (Jul 1, 2026) — 123 accounts, FINRA “bluesheet” analysis$391,367.19
FINRA declaration (Oremland) — investor losses, Dec 1, 2015 – Aug 31, 2016$400,238.03
Alternative “Modified Rescissory Method”$1,090,557.63

For scale: reporting around sentencing put about $4.9 million into Foley and his wife’s pockets across the two NanoTech companies, per regulators (Al Lewis, Business Blunders, Aug. 21, 2026) — a different measure from the NTGL-window investor-loss figures above.

How payment actually works, if restitution was ordered

Per DOJ’s restitution guidance: the U.S. Attorney’s Financial Litigation Unit enforces the judgment for 20 years from judgment plus the time incarcerated; the Clerk of Court disburses payments pro rata, only as money is actually collected; and full recovery is, in DOJ’s words, realistically very unlikely. Victims who don’t keep a current mailing address on file with VNS and the Clerk risk having their share distributed to other victims. DOJ, “Restitution Process” (updated Oct. 10, 2023).

4

What a victim who lost ~$60,000 does now

No published victim claims deadline for criminal restitution in this case was found — which is exactly why these calls matter. The system pays the victims it can identify and locate. DOJ is not the victim’s attorney; anyone pursuing this should also consult their own lawyer.

  1. Confirm you are on the government’s victim list for Case No. 1:21-cr-00019

    Call the U.S. Attorney’s Office, Northern District of Illinois (219 S. Dearborn St., Suite 500, Chicago, IL 60604) at (312) 353-5300 and ask for the Victim-Witness Coordinator / Specialist for United States v. David Foley, 21 CR 19 (Judge Seeger), plus the Financial Litigation contact. State your full name, that you are a victim, and your ~$60,000 loss. Ask three things: (a) Am I identified as a victim in VNS for this case? (b) Did the August 2026 judgment order restitution — what total, and am I on the restitution list, in what individual amount? (c) If restitution was deferred, what is the final-determination date, and what loss documentation must I file by then?

  2. Fix your VNS record today

    If you ever received a Victim Notification System letter, use your VIN/PIN at www.notify.usdoj.gov or call the VNS Call Center at 1-866-365-4968 (1-866-DOJ-4YOU) to confirm your address, phone, and email are current. If you never received a VIN/PIN, that itself is a red flag — tell the Coordinator in Step 1 and ask to be added.

  3. Document your loss in the exact form the government used

    The loss analysis that drove sentencing covered NTGL purchases and sales, December 2015 – August 2016, by brokerage account (123 accounts, $391,367.19 total). Send the Coordinator/Probation (via the Coordinator) a signed loss declaration with brokerage statements showing NTGL buys, sells, dates, quantities, and prices, plus your realized-loss calculation. Say exactly what you bought and when, and ask in writing whether your loss is included — and if not, why not.

  4. Make sure the Clerk can pay you

    Contact the Clerk’s Office, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois (Chicago) and confirm the name, mailing address, and tax/payee information it holds for you as a restitution payee — and update it whenever you move. The Clerk, not Foley, sends payments, pro rata and only as funds are collected; a stale address can send your share to other victims.

  5. Feed collection

    Give the Financial Litigation Unit anything you know about Foley’s assets, income, companies (e.g., Royal Capital, Galaxy Entertainment), or transfers — DOJ expressly invites this. You can also ask the Clerk for an Abstract of Judgment to pursue a lien yourself. Enforcement can run 20 years from judgment plus incarceration.

  6. Track the separate SEC civil case — a second, distinct pot

    In SEC v. River North Equity LLC et al., No. 1:19-cv-01711 (N.D. Ill.), River North and Edward Liceaga entered consent judgments (Aug. 26, 2025) holding them jointly and severally liable for $2.6 million, while the SEC’s claims against David Foley, Lisa Foley, and Michael Chavez remained pending as of late-2025 filings. No SEC Fair Fund or distribution claims process for this case was confirmed. Monitor the SEC case page and ask the SEC Chicago Regional Office / the USAO coordinator whether a Fair Fund is created — if one is, it will have its own claims notice and deadline, separate from criminal restitution.

  7. Watch the two deadlines that may exist

    (i) Any deferred-restitution determination date — roughly 90 days after August 19, 2026, if the court set one; and (ii) any SEC Fair Fund claims bar date, if a fund is later created. Do not assume doing nothing preserves your place.

A hard caution on the $60,000: a single $60,000 loss is more than 15% of the government’s entire $391,367.19 figure for the NTGL window. If your loss was in NTEK shares, was a direct promissory-note loan or investment, or falls outside the December 2015 – August 2016 window and methodology, you may not be inside the criminal-case loss figure at all — the indictment covered NTGL only. That is a question to put to the Coordinator in writing, not a reason to stay silent.

5

The earlier case — separate, and not to be conflated

Foley has two distinct federal criminal histories. The 72-month sentence belongs to the Chicago NTGL case above. This California case is the reason he was in prison while part of that scheme allegedly ran.

USA v. Foley et al., No. 5:09-cr-00670-EJD, and the bank-fraud indictment 11-00554 (N.D. Cal., San Jose, Judge Edward J. Davila). Foley — former Global VR CTO and former owner of NexTune Corp. d/b/a UltraCade Technologies — pleaded guilty on January 6, 2012 to conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud and conspiracy to commit bank fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1349). He admitted manufacturing counterfeit UltraCade “game packs” after Global VR had acquired the UltraCade rights and selling them as genuine, and defrauding Countrywide Home Loans of a $2,624,475 mortgage and a $374,925 HELOC by falsely claiming he was still employed at Global VR.

On January 21, 2014 he was sentenced to 24 months, a $5,000 fine, and 3 years of supervised release. Court records summarized in forum PACER posts show a recommendation he be designated to Taft CI (California) on surrender June 9, 2015; the SEC complaint states he reported to prison in June 2015 and was released from federal custody in December 2016 (BOP Register No. 13141-111; forum-quoted BOP records give a release date of 3/14/2017 including community confinement). DOJ, U.S. Attorney’s Office N.D. Cal.

One more trap for anyone searching: a Marc Foley (1st Cir. mortgage fraud, also 72 months) and a Michael Foley (Indiana methamphetamine) appear in the same search results. They are different people — a common source of false “Foley, 72 months, wire fraud” matches.

The “1% reward,” decoded

The announcement is real. The 1% is not — at least not in anything DOJ has published. On Tuesday, October 20, 2026, DOJ launched the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program (WHIP) at an Oval Office event with President Trump, VP Vance, and YouTuber Nick Shirley — whose late-2025 videos alleged fraud at Minnesota childcare centers, and who was named the program’s first recipient, at $10,000.

“If you turn somebody in and you find $1 billion, we are giving a commission to that person of 1%.” … “I will be the arbitrator of all of these decisions.” — President Trump at the WHIP launch, as reported by Reuters, Oct. 20, 2026

What was said at the launch

  • An unlimited 1% commission on recovered funds
  • Journalists could participate
  • The President would personally arbitrate disputes
  • Reuters flagged the discrepancy in the same story: DOJ described the program differently

What DOJ actually published

  • $10,000 – $30,000 per tip, in DOJ’s sole discretion
  • Paid when a tip leads to criminal charges — earlier than older programs, which pay only after final resolution
  • $25 million committed by Treasury; a 3-year pilot
  • Covers federal health-care offenses and fraud on programs/benefits run by USDA, DOL, HHS, HUD, Education, VA, the Department of War, and SBA — priority: hospice, home health, day care, special-services fraud
  • Issued under existing authority, 28 U.S.C. § 530C(b)(1)(L) — an administrative program, not a new law and not a percentage entitlement
  • Determinations are “entirely discretionary… neither appealable nor subject to judicial review” and create no enforceable rights

Eligibility, per the written terms: an individual (not a company) who voluntarily provides truthful, complete, original information in writing via the signed intake form at america.gov/fraud, and cooperates; the tip must lead to an unsealed indictment, information, or criminal complaint in a covered category. No minimum loss threshold; multiple tipsters on the same matter can each be paid. Excluded: DOJ/law-enforcement officials, employees, contractors and close household relatives; foreign officials; anyone who meaningfully participated in the crime; and information obtained by privilege, compliance/audit roles, or illegal means. Anonymous submission is possible only through an attorney, and identity must be disclosed and verified before payment. WHIP participants remain eligible for larger awards under other programs.

Bottom line: no 1% figure appears in DOJ’s press release or terms, and no statute or published rule adopting it was found. Shirley’s $10,000 first award matches the DOJ range, not 1% of any Minnesota recovery. Anyone relying on WHIP should rely on $10,000–$30,000, discretionary — not 1%. (Context Reuters noted: Minnesota’s Department of Children, Youth, and Families disputed some of Shirley’s underlying claims as misleading or exaggerated, though one Minneapolis childcare operator he profiled later pleaded guilty to fraud charges.) Reuters, Oct. 20, 2026; DOJ WHIP release; DOJ program terms.

Every reward channel, side by side

The established percentage programs are far larger than WHIP — but each only covers its own kind of fraud, and each has its own door in.

Which channel fits a fraud you know about?

Pick the kind of fraud. The match below is drawn only from the programs compared in this brief.

DOJ / FBI general tip Any federal crime, including general fraud; cyber-enabled crime via IC3. Also report to the harmed agency’s Inspector General. No general reward promised (specific wanted/fugitive rewards are separate) tips.fbi.gov, 1-800-CALL-FBI (1-800-225-5324), or ic3.gov

Mandatory 15–30% of collected proceeds if the amount in dispute exceeds $2 million (for an individual taxpayer, gross income over $200,000 in a year at issue). Below that, awards are discretionary. SEC Whistleblower Program Violations of the federal securities laws — fraud, manipulation, insider trading, Ponzi and pump-and-dump schemes, accounting/disclosure violations (Dodd-Frank § 922; Exchange Act § 21F). 10–30% of money collected — only if monetary sanctions exceed $1 million in a successful enforcement action. Form TCR at sec.gov/whistleblower; anonymous filing only through an attorney
False Claims Act qui tam False claims for payment / fraud on the U.S. government — Medicare/Medicaid billing, defense contracting, federal grants and loans (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733). 15–25% of recovery if the government intervenes; 25–30% if it declines and the relator wins alone. A civil lawsuit filed under seal in federal district court (in practice through qui tam counsel), served on DOJ, which investigates and decides whether to intervene. It is a lawsuit, not a tip form.

(a) A named victim in the Foley case

  • WHIP: No. It pays for new tips leading to criminal charges in health-care/benefit-program fraud. This case is already charged, tried, and sentenced — and it is a securities case, outside WHIP’s categories. Being a victim is not a WHIP tip.
  • SEC whistleblower: almost certainly no, for this case. It requires voluntarily provided original information that led to the enforcement action, with sanctions over $1 million. A victim who did not tip the SEC before its 2019 action cannot retroactively qualify. Genuinely new information about other, ongoing securities violations could still support a Form TCR.
  • IRS / FCA qui tam: No, on these facts. No tax underpayment is being reported and no false claim was made against the government — investors, not the Treasury, lost the money.
  • The real channels are restitution and the SEC civil case — Section 4. An FBI/DOJ tip remains available for genuinely new Foley-related criminal conduct (e.g., hidden assets — most usefully given to the Financial Litigation Unit for collection).

(b) A citizen who knows of unrelated, ongoing fraud on the government

  • Match the fraud type to the program first — percentages, routes, and first-filing/originality rules differ sharply.
  • Health-care or federal benefit-program fraud → WHIP for a fast, small, discretionary $10k–$30k at the charging stage — and in parallel consider FCA qui tam (15–30% of a potentially large recovery, but a sealed lawsuit, usually with counsel, and a wait for final resolution). WHIP eligibility does not forfeit other-program awards, but how and when you file in each program can affect the others — get whistleblower counsel before filing if a large FCA/SEC/IRS award is plausible.
  • Tax fraud over $2 million → IRS Form 211 (mandatory 15–30%). Securities fraud with likely sanctions over $1 million → SEC Form TCR (10–30%, attorney-anonymous).
  • Anything else criminal, or no award sought → FBI tips / 1-800-CALL-FBI, IC3 for online schemes, or the affected agency’s OIG hotline.
  • Do not budget on the “1%.” The codified percentage programs are IRS (26 U.S.C. § 7623), SEC (15 U.S.C. § 78u-6), and FCA (31 U.S.C. § 3730(d)). WHIP’s only written terms are $10,000–$30,000 at DOJ’s unreviewable discretion.

Still unverified — stated plainly

What this research could not confirm from public sources, as of October 23, 2026. Each of these is answerable — most by the Victim-Witness Coordinator call in Step 1, or by the written Judgment & Commitment order.

  • Final restitution in U.S. v. Foley. Whether the August 19, 2026 judgment ordered restitution, the total, the per-victim list and amounts, or a deferred-restitution date. The $391,367.19 / $400,238.03 figures are pre-sentencing government loss estimates, not confirmed orders.
  • Report-to-prison date and facility for the 72-month sentence — only contradictory forum speculation exists publicly.
  • Custody status and appeal. Whether Foley was taken into custody at sentencing or allowed to self-surrender, and whether he has appealed.
  • Blankenship’s sentence. His sentencing was reported as rescheduled to September 3, 2026 (forum reports); the outcome was not confirmed.
  • Any SEC Fair Fund in SEC v. River North, 1:19-cv-01711 — the case against David and Lisa Foley was still pending in late-2025 filings; no distribution plan was found.
  • Any legal instrument adopting a 1% whistleblower commission. None found in DOJ’s WHIP release, its terms, or its cited authority. On this record, the 1% exists only in the President’s oral remarks at the October 20, 2026 launch.
  • Whether a specific ~$60,000 loss falls inside the government’s NTGL loss figure — that depends on the security bought, the dates, and the note involved, which public sources don’t contain. It can only be settled by the Coordinator, against the victim’s own records.
